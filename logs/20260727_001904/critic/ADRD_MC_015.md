Question: How does dementia affect gait and walking?
Answer: Alzheimer's disease can affect a person's ability to walk. People with dementia may walk more slowly, shuffle their feet, or have difficulty picking up their feet when walking. Their walking may also become erratic, with quick and slow steps mixed together. Additionally, their stride length may change, and they may be unsure of where they are walking to. It is important to inform your healthcare provider if you notice any changes in their walking. Physical therapy or occupational therapy can help address these issues and prevent falls. It is also important to make adjustments to the environment to ensure safety, such as removing area rugs or toys that could cause tripping. It's worth considering that walking changes may not be solely caused by dementia, but other physical symptoms as well. Discussing with healthcare providers about suitable shoes can also be helpful. The goal is to keep the person as active as possible while ensuring their safety. Remember to reach out to your healthcare provider for guidance and support.
