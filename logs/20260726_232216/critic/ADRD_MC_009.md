Question: How can I include someone with advanced dementia in activities?
Answer: People with advanced dementia can still participate in activities that bring them joy and improve their overall well-being. It's important for us, as caregivers, to let go of our expectations about how these activities should look. Even if someone with dementia doesn't follow the rules or seems to be doing something that doesn't make sense, it's okay. What matters is if they are enjoying themselves. Let them read a book upside down or hold cards the wrong way. Even coloring outside the lines is fine. You can also introduce new activities that they haven't tried before. Remember to tap into their past and bring activities they used to enjoy into their current life. For example, if they were a golfer, they may enjoy looking at golf magazines, watching golf on TV, or playing a putting game at home. Don't underestimate the value of simply being in their presence while you engage in an activity. Pay attention to their non-verbal cues and, if they can still speak, ask for their feedback. Their calmness and enjoyment will guide you in providing meaningful activities.
