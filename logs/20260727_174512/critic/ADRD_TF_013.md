Question: What causes aggressive behavior in people with dementia?
Answer: Aggressive behavior in people with dementia, particularly Alzheimer's disease, can be a challenging issue for families. The frequency of aggression tends to increase as the disease progresses. Understanding the triggers for this behavior is crucial in managing it effectively. Disorientation can trigger agitation and aggression, even when individuals are in their own homes. Changes in the surroundings or the feeling of not being at home anymore can contribute to this behavior. Personal care activities, such as bathing or dressing, can also provoke frustration and aggression. In some cases, aggression can be a manifestation of underlying depression that individuals with Alzheimer's find difficult to express. It is important to note that aggression can also indicate pain or other medical conditions. Surprisingly, even constipation can be a potential cause. By identifying the factors or triggers that precede aggressive episodes, caregivers can better address and prevent such behavior. Taking aggression seriously is of utmost importance, as it ensures the safety of both the individual and the caregiver.

Question: Why do some people with dementia lie?
Answer: People with dementia often say things that might not be true. This is because dementia affects their memory, especially in Alzheimer's disease. When they say things that are not true, it's not really lying in the traditional sense. They might actually believe what they are saying is the truth. Their poor memory causes them to fill in the gaps with what they think might have happened or what they truly believe happened. Another reason people with dementia might lie is because they are aware that something is wrong with their brain and they have memory problems. They try to cover it up and don't want others to realize they are having trouble with their thinking. To appear as if they don't have memory problems, they make up stories and facts. It's important to understand that the person with dementia is not intentionally trying to deceive or lie, but their condition affects their perception of reality.
