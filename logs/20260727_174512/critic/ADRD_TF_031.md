Answer: Responding to anger in someone with dementia can be challenging, but understanding the reasons behind it can help. In many cases, anger is linked to delusions or hallucinations where the person believes someone is stealing from them or has harmed them. Correcting these false beliefs can often alleviate the anger. If the anger persists without an apparent cause, it's essential to uncover the root of their frustration. For example, when a person with dementia is placed in a long-term care facility, they may become angry with their family members. In these situations, healthcare providers often advise families to redirect the blame onto themselves. This way, the person with dementia may eventually forget their anger towards their family.
