What is dementia?
Dementia is an overall term for a set of symptoms that is caused by disorders that affect a person's brain.
Symptoms may include memory loss and changes in mood and behaviour, as well as difficulties with thinking
or language that are severe enough to reduce a person's ability to perform everyday tasks.
Dementia is progressive, meaning that its symptoms gradually get worse over time. There is currently no
cure for dementia. However, there are treatment options and brain healthy lifestyle choices that may slow it
down and can lead to an improved quality-of-life. Brain healthy lifestyle choices can also reduce your risk of
developing dementia.

Question: How do you treat depression in people recently diagnosed with dementia?
Answer: If someone with dementia is also experiencing depression, there are different treatment options depending on the severity of the depression. If it'sa true diagnosis of depression, medication and therapy are recommended. The level of depression can range from mild to severe. If the person is having suicidal thoughts, it's important to seek immediate help. But if the depression is more on the milder side, it may be a result of struggling with the diagnosis and not knowing what to do. In these cases, seeing a healthcare professional is still recommended to address the depressive symptoms. The healthcare provider, along with the person diagnosed with dementia and their trusted family members, can discuss and come up with strategies for treatment. Generally, therapy and medication, such as antidepressants, can be effective for individuals with dementia who have recently been diagnosed with depression.
