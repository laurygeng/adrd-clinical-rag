In dementia caregiving, **the great majority of communication — about 90% — is nonverbal**, conveyed through facial expression, body language, posture, tone of voice, and touch rather than through words. Because roughly 90% of communication is nonverbal, caregivers should pay close attention to these cues.
**People are usually not aware of the nonverbal messages they are sending.** Nonverbal communication is largely unconscious, so a caregiver's own facial expressions, posture, and tone may convey feelings — such as impatience, stress, or warmth — without the caregiver realizing it.
**Once you learn how to look for nonverbal messages, you will better understand what a person living with dementia is trying to express.** As verbal ability declines, people living with dementia rely increasingly on nonverbal communication, so reading these cues reveals their needs and feelings.

**People living with dementia forget about 90% of new information within 1 to 5 minutes** of receiving it, because short-term memory is impaired. Caregivers should therefore repeat information, keep it simple, and not rely on the person retaining newly given details.
**Emotionally spiced (emotionally expressive) nonverbal communication is effective in improving retention of new information** for people living with dementia. Pairing information with warm, emotionally meaningful nonverbal cues helps the information be remembered.
