...Question: Can a dementia diagnosis be incorrect?
Answer: Yes, a dementia diagnosis can be incorrect. It's important to understand that dementia is not a specific disease itself, but rather a term used to describe a functional status involving cognitive impairments. There are different levels of cognitive impairments, which range from subjective cognitive impairment (when there are impairments but they don't affect daily life) to mild cognitive impairment (where activities of daily living may be affected but not instrumental activities like driving or managing finances) to major neurocognitive impairment or dementia (where both activities of daily living and instrumental activities are impacted). Determining the specific cause of the functional impairment, such as Alzheimer's, Lewy body disease, frontal temporal lobe dementia, or vascular dementia, can be challenging. Sometimes, a person may initially be diagnosed with one condition, like Alzheimer's, only to later discover that they actually have a different underlying cause of their cognitive impairments. It is through ongoing evaluation and the gathering of more information that a more accurate diagnosis can be made.

Question: Why do some people with dementia lie?
Answer: People with dementia often say things that might not be true. This is because dementia affects their memory, especially in Alzheimer's disease. When they say things that are not true, it's not really lying in the traditional sense. They might actually believe what they are saying is the truth. Their poor memory causes them to fill in the gaps with what they think might have happened or what they truly believe happened. Another reason people with dementia might lie is because they are aware that something is wrong with their brain and they have memory problems. They try to cover it up and don't want others to realize they are having trouble with their thinking. To appear as if they don't have memory problems, they make up stories and facts. It's important to understand that the person with dementia is not intentionally trying to deceive or lie, but their condition affects their perception of reality.

What is dementia?
