It's important for caregivers to recognize the toll that this role can take on their mental and emotional well-being. Often, caregivers find themselves overwhelmed by the behavioral symptoms of dementia and may feel like they are constantly on duty, 24/7, with no respite. One way to cope with these behavioral symptoms is to consider how much time is being devoted to caregiving. If you are the sole caregiver and are with the person with dementia all the time, it's crucial to understand that you are asking your brain to do the impossible with inadequate support. It's okay to acknowledge that you need a break and cannot do it all alone. Limiting the amount of time spent caregiving can have a positive impact on both you and the person with dementia. By acknowledging that you need help, you can become more of a partner in their care instead of solely shouldering the responsibility.
