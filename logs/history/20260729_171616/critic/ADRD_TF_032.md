Question: What are some easy activities for someone with dementia?
Answer: When it comes to engaging someone who is living with dementia, it's important to choose activities that are simple and easy. Even basic tasks like doing laundry or making a sandwich can be too complicated for someone with moderate to late stage dementia. These tasks require a lot of mental effort and can become overwhelming. Instead, it's helpful to think about activities that are one or two steps and can provide a break for the caregiver. For example, listening to music can be a soothing and enjoyable activity. Stringing beads onto a string or sorting items like silverware can also be engaging and not too challenging. Folding towels is another great activity that can give a sense of purpose and accomplishment. For instance, one person I work with has their spouse put already clean towels into the dryer for a quick spin, and then they work together to fold them. This allows the caregiver to feel helpful and involved in the household tasks. These activities provide both mental stimulation and a sense of meaning for individuals living with dementia. They also allow caregivers to have a few minutes of respite to attend to their own needs.
