This behavior is known as "shadowing," and it involves the person positioning themselves behind you and occasionally touching you. They may also ask repetitive questions or say the same things over and over again. It's important to understand that they are not doing this on purpose, but rather because they see you as someone they trust to guide them and keep them safe. However, if this behavior continues, it can be tiring and frustrating for you as the caregiver. To address this issue, it may be helpful to introduce a third person into the situation who can take on some of the caregiving responsibilities. This person should be someone the person with dementia can trust and who understands where to go and what to do. By building this triad early on, you can provide the person with dementia with another person to follow and rely on, giving you the opportunity to take a break and have your own needs met. If necessary, you may need to seek help from a volunteer, a family member, or even hire someone to give you some respite time.
