Question: Can Alzheimer's disease be prevented?
Answer: Prevention of Alzheimer's disease specifically is not yet possible, as we still do not fully understand how to prevent the accumulation of amyloid plaques and tau protein tangles in the brain. However, there is evidence that a healthy lifestyle can delay or prevent the symptoms of Alzheimer's disease, which is ultimately what we call a dementia syndrome. Maintaining a healthy lifestyle involves eating a well-balanced diet, getting regular aerobic exercise that increases blood flow to the brain, and ensuring you get enough sleep, including both light, deep, and REM sleep. Managing other chronic conditions, such as diabetes or high blood pressure, is also important for brain health. It's not just physical health that plays a role, but social engagement is also beneficial. Engaging in healthy activities with people you enjoy spending time with can have positive effects on your brain. While activities like crossword puzzles and Sudoku may improve specific cognitive skills, they don't necessarily transfer to real-life tasks. So while it's good to keep your brain active, it's important to have a holistic approach to brain health.

Studies show that engaging in a moderate amount of aerobic exercise for at least 30 minutes, five days a week, or some form of resistance training, can be beneficial. So, the bottom line is that being sedentary is a risk factor for Alzheimer's, while regular physical activity can help reduce that risk. Although there is no exact formula, incorporating moderate aerobic exercise or resistance training into your routine can be beneficial for your brain health....
