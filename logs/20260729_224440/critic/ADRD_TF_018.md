Answer: Delirium is a common problem for individuals with cognitive impairments, such as dementia. It is characterized by fluctuations in alertness or arousal, which refers to how awake and attentive a person is to their surroundings and themselves. When someone experiences delirium, they may be confused and unaware of their location or the activities they are engaged in. Sometimes, they may even attribute themselves to the wrong place or task. This state of confusion is usually temporary, lasting from a few minutes to several days or even weeks as the person recovers. Delirium is an important issue to be aware of when caring for someone with cognitive impairments.
