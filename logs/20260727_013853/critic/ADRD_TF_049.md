...Question: What's the difference between dementia and Alzheimer's disease?
Answer: Dementia and Alzheimer's disease are related but different terms. Dementia refers to cognitive difficulties, such as memory problems, that are significant enough to impact daily tasks like paying bills or cooking. Alzheimer's disease is the most common cause of dementia, but there are other causes, such as dementia with Lewy bodies, frontotemporal dementia, and vascular dementia. Some people may have changes in their brain associated with Alzheimer's disease, like the buildup of proteins called amyloid and tau, but they may not have enough cognitive impairment to meet the criteria for dementia. In these cases, it may be referred to as mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer's disease. However, it's important to note that there is debate in the field about whether these individuals should be considered to have Alzheimer's disease.
