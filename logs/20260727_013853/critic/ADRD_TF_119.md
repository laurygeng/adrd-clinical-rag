Dementia care planning includes the development and implementation of a **caregiver stress prevention bundle**. The caregiver stress prevention bundle includes the following components:
- **Weekly respite care:** at least **8 consecutive hours per week** of respite care, during which the caregiver is not providing care for their loved one living with dementia and is instead focusing on their own emotional health. As part of caregiver self-care, the caregiver should spend at least 8 consecutive hours away from caregiving per week.
- **Monthly support group:** attending a **monthly support group**, with or without their loved one living with dementia.
- **Problem-solving skills development:** developing **problem-solving skills** to help the caregiver cope with **their own emotional and physical stress**, and to manage the **cognitive, functional, behavioral, and psychological disabilities** of their loved one living with dementia. Problem-solving skills development is a component of the caregiver stress prevention bundle.

Question: How can I continue to care for myself while caring for someone living with dementia?
Answer: When caring for someone with dementia, it's crucial to also take care of yourself. Your own well-being is directly linked to your ability to provide effective care. Taking care of yourself means getting enough rest, eating nourishing meals, and maintaining your overall health. It's important to schedule regular check-ups with your primary care physician and take any prescribed medications. Joining a caregiver support group can provide valuable support and give you permission to take breaks and prioritize your own needs. It may also be helpful to have your own mental health provider to guide you through the different stages of caregiving and offer a safe outlet for difficult emotions. Couples counseling or counseling with your loved one who has dementia can also be beneficial for a period of time. Remember, every step you take to protect your own health and well-being will contribute to a higher quality of life for both you and the person you are caring for.
