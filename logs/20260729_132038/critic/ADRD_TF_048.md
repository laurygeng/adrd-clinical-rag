...Question: Can a dementia diagnosis be incorrect?
Answer: Yes, a dementia diagnosis can be incorrect. It's important to understand that dementia is not a specific disease itself, but rather a term used to describe a functional status involving cognitive impairments. There are different levels of cognitive impairments, which range from subjective cognitive impairment (when there are impairments but they don't affect daily life) to mild cognitive impairment (where activities of daily living may be affected but not instrumental activities like driving or managing finances) to major neurocognitive impairment or dementia (where both activities of daily living and instrumental activities are impacted). Determining the specific cause of the functional impairment, such as Alzheimer's, Lewy body disease, frontal temporal lobe dementia, or vascular dementia, can be challenging.
