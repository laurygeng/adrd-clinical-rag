...Answer: Dementia is a complex medical condition, particularly when it comes to the genetics of Alzheimer's disease. In about 1% of cases, Alzheimer's disease is inherited as an autosomal dominant condition. This means that if you have a specific gene, your chances of developing the disease are nearly 100%. These families are easily identified because the disease usually starts before the age of 60, and sometimes even earlier. On the other hand, the more common form of Alzheimer's disease is late onset Alzheimer's disease, which occurs after the age of 60 or 65. In this case, there are genes that slightly increase the risk of developing the disease, but the increase is minimal. So if you have a sibling with Alzheimer's disease, your own risk only goes up a little bit. To put it into perspective, having a family member with heart troubles increases your own risk of developing heart disease to some extent. But if you have good cholesterol levels, normal blood pressure, don't have diabetes, and don't smoke, that one factor alone only raises your risk of heart disease slightly. The same sort of math applies to the risk of developing Alzheimer's disease. In conclusion, while having a sibling with Alzheimer's disease may slightly increase your risk, it is important to focus on maintaining a healthy lifestyle and managing other risk factors to reduce your overall chances of developing the disease....
